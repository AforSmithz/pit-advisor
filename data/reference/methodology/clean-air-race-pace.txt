Clean-air race pace: how it is fitted

Clean-air race pace is the foundation metric. A raw lap time is not a measurement of a car's
pace: it carries fuel load, tyre age, the compound on the car, the traffic ahead and whatever
the track was doing at the time. The estimator removes what it can and refuses to guess at the
rest.

Laps are excluded before anything is fitted, and every exclusion carries a named reason code
that is counted and published rather than hidden. The codes are no_lap_time, off_regime,
no_compound, deleted, out_lap, in_lap, opening_laps, track_not_green, inaccurate, gap_unknown
and traffic. In plain terms: a lap with no time, a lap from the other weather regime, a lap on
a compound FastF1 could not name, a lap deleted for track limits, the lap into and out of the
pits, the first two laps of the race, any lap not run under green flag conditions, a lap FastF1
itself flags as inaccurate, a lap where the gap to the car ahead is unknown, and a lap run
within 1500 ms of the car ahead. That last one is the big one and it is deliberate: a lap in
dirty air measures the gap, not the pace, and if the exclusion rate looks low the filter is not
working.

Wet and dry are different regimes, not fast and slow versions of the same thing, so each is
fitted separately and compared elsewhere. A session is fitted only when at least two drivers
have five or more surviving clean laps.

What is fitted is one model per session, not one per driver and not one per stint. Each driver
gets his own intercept; the tyre-age slope, the race-progress slope and the compound offsets are
shared across the field. The plan originally specified a per-driver-stint fit and that model
cannot be estimated at all: within a stint the laps remaining and the lap number in the stint are
the same variable with the sign flipped, so the two slopes are perfectly collinear, and the
design goes singular. Rather than let a least-squares routine return a minimum-norm answer that
looks like a lap time and is not one, the fit checks the rank of the design first and refuses.

The fit is Huber, tuned at 1.345, which is insurance against a driver with a genuinely heavier
tail than his teammate rather than a general accuracy improvement. The design is centred on its
own centroid, where the intercept is best determined, and shifted analytically back to the
display reference of a fresh tyre at the end of the race.

The intercept, corrected that way, is the driver's clean-air pace. It is expressed as a
percentage off a session benchmark, which is the mean of the best three corrected estimates
rather than the single fastest, because the minimum of twenty noisy estimates is an extreme
order statistic and carries a race-specific bias large enough to break the cross-weekend
comparison the normalisation exists to provide.

Two things make a race produce no dry fit at all, and they look identical in a count. Some races
have too few clean laps left: usually a wet race with nothing in the dry regime, sometimes a dry
race at a circuit where DRS trains put nearly every lap inside the traffic threshold. Others have
plenty of laps but a confounded design, because after filtering each driver's surviving laps come
from a single stint. Where dropping the race-progress slope restores the rank, the fit is redone
without it and the progress term is reported as absent; where it does not, the race is refused.
Loosening the traffic threshold would recover races by reintroducing exactly the bias the filter
exists to remove, so the threshold is not the fix.
