Backtesting and calibration: how the forecast is judged

A forecast that is never scored against what happened is decoration. The rule this project
holds itself to is that the model is only allowed to claim value if it beats a dumb baseline
out of sample, and the dashboard leads with the calibration page rather than with predictions.

The backtest walks the calendar forward. Every race is predicted using only what was known
before it was run: ratings fitted as of that date, decay weights computed as of that date, and
a weather forecast rather than an observation. There is no shuffling and there are no random
splits, because a random split over a time series lets the model see the future.

Three baselines are scored on exactly the same races. Grid position only, championship standings
only, and last race result. Grid position is the hard one to beat and it is the honest
comparison: most of the information about who wins a Formula 1 race is already in where they
start.

The scores are multiclass log loss and Brier over classified finishing position, plus expected
calibration error for the win, podium and points markets. Intervals come from a bootstrap
resampled at the race level, never at the driver level. Twenty drivers in one race share a
circuit, a safety car and often a strategy, so treating them as twenty independent observations
produces intervals several times too narrow and would let noise read as a result.

The difference against each baseline is resampled over the same races, so the paired comparison
is on the same events rather than on two independent samples. An interval on that difference
which straddles zero means the holdout does not separate the two, and that is reported as not
separated rather than as a win.

The phase this work belongs to passes whether or not the model beats its baselines. What it has
to do is measure honestly. A model that beats the baselines by a wide margin is treated as a bug
until proven otherwise: the first things checked are whether the holdout is really time-forward,
whether grid position has leaked in as a feature in disguise, and whether any post-race
information reached the features.

The current scores, the intervals and the reliability curves live in the backtest artifacts and
in the calibration view. They are not repeated here, because a document that carries a number
the artifacts own will eventually disagree with them.
