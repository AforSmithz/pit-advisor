Track fit: how a team's suitability to a circuit is estimated

A circuit taxonomy, hand-maintained, describes every circuit on the calendar with the things
that actually drive performance: downforce level, traction demand, braking energy, top-speed
sensitivity, surface abrasiveness, kerb severity, altitude, pit-lane loss, DRS gain, overtaking
difficulty and the historical safety-car rate. Every field is either a published constant or a
measured aggregate; none of it is a hand-written opinion about how a car will go.

Track fit is estimated two ways and the two are shown side by side rather than blended into one
number. The first regresses a team's historical corrected pace on the circuit feature vector and
predicts at the target circuit. The second scores circuits by cosine similarity over the same
feature vector, z-scored because altitude spans thousands of metres and a demand band spans four
points, and weights a team's history at the nearest circuits by both similarity and recency.

A circuit is treated as its own nearest neighbour. A team's record at this track is the
strongest available evidence about this track, and dropping it in favour of lookalikes throws
away the best data in the set.

A reprofiled circuit is a different track wearing the same name, so races run before the change
are not treated as evidence about the current layout. The taxonomy records when a layout became
comparable to its present form and history is cut there.

Where the two estimators disagree, the disagreement is displayed rather than resolved. Two
methods that diverge are telling you the estimate is weak, which is information the reader
needs and a blended average would hide.
