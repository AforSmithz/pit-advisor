Driver form: how the rating separates the driver from the car

A driver's results are mostly a statement about his car. Isolating the driver is the hard part
of the problem, and the instrument for it is the teammate comparison: two drivers in the same
car, at the same circuit, in the same conditions, on the same weekend. Everything that is a
property of the machine is common to both and cancels in the difference.

The rating is built from intra-team gaps, in corrected race pace and in qualifying time, fitted
jointly so that driver effects and car effects are estimated together rather than one being
assumed away. Each gap is weighted by how long ago it happened, on an exponential decay with a
half-life of five events. Five events is roughly a third of a season: recent form dominates,
last year still counts for something, and three seasons ago is close to nothing.

The decay weights are computed as of the prediction date and only as of the prediction date.
This is the single easiest place in the whole project to leak the future into a rating, and a
calibration curve that looks too good on the training window is the symptom.

Every rating is published with a 95% interval and the number of samples behind it, and both are
meant to be read. A driver with three races does not get a confident rating, and the interval
is what says so. A rating whose interval spans zero is a driver this method cannot distinguish
from his teammate, which is a real finding rather than a missing number.

The same machinery produces the qualifying-to-race trend, on the same decay, from the same
teammate deltas. The wet-weather delta is built the same way from sessions FastF1 marks as wet,
and its interval is usually wide, because wet sessions are rare and a driver may have very few.

The rating is a pace and gap measurement. It is not a prediction of finishing position, it does
not know about reliability, and it does not know whether the car has been upgraded since. Those
live in other parts of the system and are combined only in the simulation.
