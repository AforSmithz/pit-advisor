Qualifying to race delta: how a Saturday car is told apart from a Sunday car

Some cars are set up to produce one lap and some are set up to run a race distance. The delta
between a driver's corrected qualifying pace and his corrected race pace is the signal that
separates them, and it is one of the few genuinely actionable things in a pre-weekend view.

Qualifying times cannot be compared raw across the segments. A driver knocked out in Q1 never
ran on the rubbered-in track the Q3 runners had, and charging him for that would read as a slow
car when it is a track that improved. The estimator measures track evolution from within-driver
medians chained across the segments, using drivers who appear in both, and corrects each best
lap for the segment it was set in. Where too few drivers ran in both segments to measure the
offset, the correction is not applied rather than invented.

The race side is the clean-air pace described in the clean-air race pace note, so both sides of
the delta are corrected quantities normalised the same way.

One weekend's delta is a strategy call, a tyre that went off, or a safety car at the wrong
moment. A standing gap between Saturday and Sunday across many weekends is a property of the
car. So the delta is reported as a trend over history with the same five-event decay the form
rating uses, not as a single weekend's number, and it carries its interval.
